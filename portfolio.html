Part I

Architectural design and visualisation

Residential and community projects taken from 2D layout through to 3D model and presentation render.

01 · Residential, multi-storey AutoCAD · SketchUp

Five-storey residential building

2D planning and 3D visualisation for a five-storey residential block. The façade is organised around a central circulation bay finished in deep red, which separates two mirrored apartment stacks and carries the stair and lift windows the full height of the building.

Each apartment opens onto a projecting balcony closed with an ornamental laser-cut screen. Repeating the screen on every floor gives the elevation its rhythm while providing shade and privacy to rooms facing the street, which matters on a narrow urban plot.

Exterior visualisation and a walkthrough animation were prepared for client presentation.

02 · Residential, duplex AutoCAD · SketchUp

Duplex house

Full architectural planning for a two-storey duplex, from the 2D house layout through to a detailed 3D model. The work covered space planning, the staircase and internal circulation, and the arrangement of rooms across both levels.

The exterior is deliberately classical: twin-height columns framing an arched entrance, a balustraded first-floor balcony, moulded cornices and a pedimented centrepiece. Stone cladding on the lower storey grounds the composition against the rendered upper walls.

Façade lighting was modelled as part of the design rather than added afterwards, and the evening render was produced for client presentation.

03 · Residential, single storey AutoCAD · SketchUp

Single-storey residence

A one-storey house planned around a flat roofline and a wide street-facing frontage. The entry wall carries a decorative laser-cut metal screen set behind full-height glazing and framed by a timber pivot door, filtering direct sun off the glass while keeping the living spaces bright.

It is a deliberately quieter, more contemporary counterpoint to the ornamental duplex above. The composition relies on proportion and a single strong material gesture rather than applied classical detail, and the plot is laid out with the parking bay to one side so the garden frontage stays uninterrupted.

04 · Residential, contemporary villa SketchUp

Contemporary villa with gatehouse

A two-storey villa set in a walled compound, modelled from massing through to a landscaped presentation view. The plan breaks the house into linked volumes: a barrel-vaulted wing, a glazed corner room that reads as a lantern from outside, and a flat-roofed block carrying a pool at first-floor level.

A separate gatehouse holds the entrance and gives the compound a formal address. The two images show the same scheme at the two stages that matter most in practice — the working model used to resolve geometry, and the rendered view used to explain the result to a client who does not read drawings.

05 · Community SketchUp

Eidgah prayer ground and gateway

An open-air Eidgah: a walled prayer ground used twice a year by the whole community, which makes it an unusual design problem. It must hold a very large congregation on two days and read well as a public landmark on the other three hundred and sixty-three.

The scheme sets a tiered minaret at one corner as the visible marker, a domed mihrab at the centre of the qibla wall, and arched gateways at each end to manage arrival and dispersal of the crowd. Rows of palms give shade along the circulation edges without obstructing the prayer lines.

Part II

Infrastructure and master planning

Work at the scale where civil engineering and planning meet: site-wide layouts and structural infrastructure.

06 · Master planning SketchUp

Institutional campus master plan

A whole-site model for an institutional campus: a multi-storey academic block at the centre, hostel and administrative buildings to either side, a gateway structure, and a full-size playing field laid out in front of the teaching buildings.

The interesting constraint was water. Two canals run along the site boundary, so the plan had to place bridge crossings where they would carry the heaviest pedestrian flow at class change, and keep the road network continuous around them. Setting the buildings back from the field also gives the main block a clear frontage.

07 · Infrastructure SketchUp

Multi-span girder bridge

A long multi-span crossing modelled in three dimensions: deck, girders, pier spacing, carriageway markings, footway and lighting columns, set over a wide stretch of water.

Modelling a bridge is a useful discipline because almost nothing about it is free. Pier spacing is set by span economy and by what the river will allow, deck width by traffic, and lighting spacing by the lux level the road class requires. The model is a way of checking that those separate requirements still agree with one another once drawn at full length.

Part III

Working drawings

The drawings a building is actually constructed from: dimensioned layouts, furniture and fixture plans, and electrical services.

08 · Technical drafting AutoCAD

Architectural and electrical working drawings

Working drawings prepared in AutoCAD: dimensioned floor plans, sanitary and kitchen fixture layouts, furniture plans, and the electrical set — conduit runs, light-load distribution and fitting positions.

Producing the architectural and electrical layers for the same building is where coordination problems surface. A conduit run and a beam want the same space; a switch position assumes a door swings a particular way. Those conflicts are cheap to resolve on a drawing and expensive to resolve on site, which is the entire argument for drawing them properly first.

The third drawing is a coloured presentation plan with each room labelled by area, used to explain a layout to a client without asking them to read dimensions.

Part IV

GIS and cadastral mapping

Land-records digitisation for national zoning work — the strand of my professional practice closest to the environmental assessment research I want to continue.

09 · GIS, cadastral mapping ArcGIS · ArcMap · Google Earth

Cadastral land mapping and GIS digitisation

Plan Bangla Consultant Firm (PBCF)  ·  Study area: Tolatala Mouza, Akhaura Upazila, Brahmanbaria District

Converting paper Mouza sheets — the traditional cadastral maps that record who owns which plot — into clean digital geodatabases usable for national land-zoning work.

The method

  • Georeferencing. Each scanned Mouza sheet is tied to high-resolution satellite imagery using identifiable ground control points, then spatially adjusted to bring it onto the real coordinate system.
  • Edge-matching. Adjacent sheets rarely agree at their shared border, because each was surveyed and drawn separately. Boundary mismatches have to be reconciled so that a plot split across two sheets becomes one polygon, not two overlapping halves.
  • Vectorisation. Plots, roads and watercourses are digitised as separate layers — the second image shows those vectors with the imagery switched off.
  • Topological correction. Polygons must share edges exactly, with no slivers, gaps or overlaps. A cadastral database with topology errors produces land disputes, so this stage governs whether the data is usable at all.
  • Plot-level overlay. The finished layer is published over current imagery with each parcel carrying its cadastral number, as in the third image.
Why this connects to my research. A cadastral database is the base layer for almost any environmental decision taken at land-parcel scale: where industry may discharge, which plots flood, how land use is changing around a river. The water-quality modelling I did as an undergraduate had no such base layer, and the analysis was weaker for it. Building these databases is how the spatial side of environmental assessment actually gets done.
