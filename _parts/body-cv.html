Personal statement

Civil Engineering graduate with four years of laboratory research training across structural, geotechnical and environmental engineering, complemented by five years of professional practice in sustainability assessment, construction supervision and management-system auditing. My undergraduate thesis on rice husk ash as a partial cement replacement established, over 54 concrete specimens, that 5 % replacement raises 28-day compressive strength by 21.8 % while lowering material cost, while earlier work applied artificial neural network models to forecast industrial impacts on urban river water quality. Together these established the research direction I intend to pursue at postgraduate level.

Professionally, I have progressed from site engineering to supervising multi-site infrastructure projects, while conducting ISO 9001, 14001 and 45001 audits, energy and ETP assessments, and GRI-standard sustainability reporting for national industrial groups and international development partners including GIZ and BGMEA. This combination of laboratory research and field implementation has shaped my central academic interest: translating sustainable materials and environmental assessment methods into practice in climate-vulnerable, rapidly urbanising countries such as Bangladesh.

I am now seeking admission to a Master's programme in Environmental Science or a closely related field, with a research focus on sustainable construction materials, environmental impact assessment and climate-resilient urban development. My longer-term objective is to contribute to evidence-based environmental policy and infrastructure practice in the Global South, and subsequently to an academic and research career in this field.

Full methods and figures
April 2021 – June 2022 · Dhaka

Undergraduate Researcher, Structural Engineering Laboratory

Presidency University · Supervisor: Susmita Majumder

Thesis: Utilisation of Rice Husk Ash as Partial Replacement of Cement for Production of Sustainable Concrete

  • Investigated sustainable concrete production through partial cement replacement with rice husk ash (RHA), as part of a six-member group thesis.
  • Cast, cured and compression-tested 54 cylindrical specimens across six replacement levels (0–25 %) and three curing ages (7, 14 and 28 days) at a 1 : 1.5 : 3 mix ratio, W/C 0.45.
  • Established that 5 % replacement raises 28-day compressive strength by 21.8 % (2,724 → 3,317 psi), with strength declining sharply beyond 10 % replacement.
  • Characterised the ash as strongly siliceous (62.3 % SiO2 against 20.5 % for the cement replaced), confirming the pozzolanic basis of the strength gain.
  • Quantified the accompanying trade-off: material cost fell by up to 6.3 % while water absorption rose from 2.63 % to 4.32 %, fixing the practical window at 5–10 %.
