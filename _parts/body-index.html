About

Laboratory research, carried into field practice

My research direction was set in the undergraduate laboratory. Between 2018 and 2022 I worked on four distinct projects: artificial neural network models forecasting industrial impacts on urban river water quality; two successive studies on reclaimed asphalt pavement in granular pile foundations, modelled in PLAXIS 3D; and finally my thesis on rice husk ash as a partial cement replacement, in which 54 concrete specimens established that 5 % replacement raises 28-day compressive strength by 21.8 % while lowering material cost.

Since graduating I have spent five years applying those methods in the field: conducting ISO 9001, 14001 and 45001 management system audits, energy and effluent treatment plant assessments, and GRI-standard sustainability reporting for national industrial groups and international development partners including GIZ and BGMEA. I now supervise multi-site civil infrastructure projects at Plan Bangla Consultant Firm.

That combination of measurement in the laboratory and implementation on site is what I want to bring to postgraduate research. I am seeking admission to an M.Sc. programme in Environmental Science or a closely related field, with a focus on sustainable construction materials, environmental impact assessment and climate-resilient urban development. My longer-term objective is a research career supporting evidence-based environmental policy and infrastructure practice in the Global South.

Environmental impact assessment

Quantitative assessment of industrial pressures on surface water and air, data-driven forecasting of environmental indicators, and the regulatory frameworks that translate assessment into enforcement.

Ground improvement & foundations

Granular pile systems in soft soils, the reuse of reclaimed asphalt pavement as a load-bearing fill, geotextile reinforcement, and finite element modelling of foundation behaviour.

Climate-resilient urban development
